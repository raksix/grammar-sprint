# B1-01 Present Perfect

## Goal

- Use have/has + past participle (V3) to talk about past actions with present results.
- Choose correctly between ever/never/just/already/yet and for/since.
- TR: Gecmiste olup sonucu bugunu etkileyen olaylari anlatmak.

## Rule in 60 seconds

- Form: **have/has + V3** (I have seen, she has gone).
- Use it for: life experience, recent news with present result, unfinished time (today, this week).
- TR: Ne zaman oldugu degil, sonuc onemliyse present perfect kullan.

## Forms

| Subject | Affirmative | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|---|
| I / you / we / they | I have finished | I have not (haven't) finished | Have you finished? |
| he / she / it | She has finished | She has not (hasn't) finished | Has she finished? |

- Past participle: regular + -ed (worked), irregular must be memorised (gone, seen, eaten, written).
- TR: Duzensiz fiillerin 3. halini ezberle: go-went-gone, see-saw-seen.
- Time markers:
  - `ever` (questions: Have you ever...?), `never` (I have never...).
  - `just` (a moment ago), `already` (sooner than expected), `yet` (questions + negatives, end of sentence).
  - `for` + duration (for 3 years), `since` + starting point (since 2020).
  - TR: for + sure, since + baslangic noktasi.

```text
have/has + V3
Have you EVER eaten sushi? — No, I have NEVER tried it.
I have JUST finished. / I have ALREADY seen it. / I haven't called YET.
She has lived here FOR 5 years / SINCE 2020.
```

## 3 classic traps

### 1. Present perfect with a finished past time word

- Wrong: I have seen him yesterday.
- Right: I saw him yesterday.
- TR: yesterday, last night, in 2019 gibi bitmis zaman varsa past simple kullan.

### 2. For vs since swap

- Wrong: I have worked here since 5 years.
- Right: I have worked here for 5 years / since 2020.
- TR: for + sure (5 years), since + tarih/an (2020, Monday, I was a child).

### 3. Gone vs been

- `gone` = went and is still there. `been` = went and came back.
- He has gone to Ankara (he is there now). He has been to Ankara (he visited before).
- TR: gone = orada, been = gidip donmus.

## 10 example sentences

1. I have just finished my homework. — Odevimi az once bitirdim.
2. She has already seen that film twice. — O filmi iki kez izledi bile.
3. Have you ever tried Turkish coffee? — Hic Turk kahvesi denedin mi?
4. I have never been to Izmir. — Hic Izmir'e gitmedim.
5. They haven't called yet. — Henuz aramadilar.
6. We have lived in Bursa since 2018. — 2018'den beri Bursa'da yasiyoruz.
7. He has worked here for ten years. — On yildir burada calisiyor.
8. The bus hasn't arrived yet. — Otobus henuz gelmedi.
9. She has just lost her keys. — Anahtarlarini az once kaybetti. (sonuc: su an yok)
10. Have you finished your project yet? — Projeni bitirdin mi henuz?

## Quick check

1. I ___ (never / see) snow. → I have never seen snow.
2. She ___ (work) here ___ 2021. → She has worked here since 2021.
3. They ___ (just / arrive). → They have just arrived.
4. ___ you ___ (eat) yet? → Have you eaten yet?
5. I saw him yesterday. (Why not present perfect? → finished time word.)
6. gone or been? "He has ___ to London, he is there now." → gone.
7. Fix: "I have done it last week." → I did it last week.
8. He has lived here ___ five years. (for/since) → for.
